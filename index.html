Proof & Genocidal Intent Reference — The Gambia v. Myanmar (19 Nov 2026)

1. Three-Tiered Standards of Proof Para. 80

Convention Obligation Applicable Standard Litigation Application
Article III Acts
(Genocide, conspiracy, incitement, attempt, complicity)		 "Fully conclusive" Highest evidentiary threshold. Applies both to underlying actus reus (Art. II) and specific intent (dolus specialis) attributable to the State.
Articles I, IV, VI
(Duties to prevent, investigate, punish)		 "High level of certainty" Appropriate to the seriousness of the allegation, yet less demanding than the "fully conclusive" bar for direct Art. III commission.
Article V
(Domestic implementing legislation)		 Statutory interpretation Assessed by interpreting the Respondent's domestic penal statutes to verify whether all Art. III elements are criminalized without gaps.

2. Burden Flexibility & Corfu Channel Non-Co-operation Para. 79; Paras. 128–131

Flexible Application Without Reversal (Para. 79): The party alleging a fact bears the burden of proving it, but the Court applies this rule flexibly where the Respondent exercises exclusive territorial control and denies access to independent investigators.

Corfu Channel Inferences (Paras. 128–131): Where a Respondent refuses territorial access to UN fact-finding bodies and leaves formal investigative requests unanswered (e.g., 26 IIMM requests), the Applicant may be allowed a "more liberal recourse to inferences of fact" regarding events within the Respondent's exclusive knowledge.

3. "Only Reasonable Inference" & Coexisting Objectives Paras. 443, 451–467

Coexistence Rule (Para. 443, 9–6): Genocidal intent (dolus specialis) "may exist alongside other intentions, provided that such intentions are not incompatible with the existence of genocidal intent." Military, security, or counter-insurgency goals do not automatically negate genocidal intent.

Eliminating Alternative Inferences (Paras. 451–467): Courts must test whether a pattern of conduct is equally explained by counter-insurgency (paras. 451–453), forced displacement (paras. 454–462), or collective punishment (paras. 463–466). Where forces attack fleeing civilians, sink escape vessels, and mine borders rather than organizing evacuations, forced displacement supports rather than negates genocidal intent (paras. 457–460).
