Protected Ethnical Group (Article II Threshold)

The Rohingya constitute a distinct protected “ethnical group” under Article II based on shared language, religion, and heritage; domestic statelessness under Myanmar’s 1982 Citizenship Law cannot strip Convention protection ().

ESTABLISHED

Art. II(a) — Killing Members of the Group

Viewing in PDF
Jump to Para. 182 ↗

Fully conclusive proof of systematic mass killings across northern Rakhine—including Min Gyi (Tula Toli), Chut Pyin, Maung Nu, and Gu Dar Pyin (). The Court accepted the estimate of ~10,000 Rohingya killed as credibly supported by corroborated demographic and eyewitness evidence ().

ESTABLISHED

Art. II(b) — Causing Serious Bodily or Mental Harm

Viewing in PDF
Jump to Para. 231 ↗

Widespread mass gang rape and sexual & gender-based violence against Rohingya women and girls, severe torture, and deliberate brutalization of children inflicted grave, lasting trauma contributing to group destruction ().

ESTABLISHED

Art. II(c) — Conditions of Life Calculated for Physical Destruction

Viewing in PDF
Jump to Para. 264 ↗

Established through the cumulative effect of the 2017 clearance operations: systematic razing of villages, destruction of crops and livestock, and deliberate denial of food, shelter, and medical relief calculated to bring about physical destruction ().

NUANCED / BIOLOGICAL RENEWAL

Art. II(d) — Measures Intended to Prevent Births

Viewing in PDF
Jump to Para. 298 ↗

Clarified that Article II(d) encompasses both physical and mental measures impairing a group’s capacity to renew itself—including trauma from systematic sexual violence, sex-separation, and targeting of pregnant women—while noting strict proof of reproductive intent ().

NOT ESTABLISHED

Art. II(e) — Forcibly Transferring Children of the Group

Viewing in PDF
Jump to Para. 315 ↗

The legal threshold for forcible transfer of children to another group under Article II(e) was not met on the record, and The Gambia did not press a standalone finding under this subparagraph ().

Genocidal Intent (Dolus Specialis)

Landmark Para. 443 Coexistence Rule & 4 circumstantial pillars of State intent

Landmark Rule
Landmark Para. 443 Coexistence Rule Viewing in PDF
Jump to Para. 443 ↗
“Genocidal intent need not be the sole intention of a State; it may coexist alongside military or counter-insurgency objectives, provided those objectives are not incompatible with an intent to destroy the group in whole or in part.”

Adopting observations from the 11 intervening States (), the Court ruled that concurrent security or counter-insurgency aims do not defeat dolus specialis where destructive means confirm genocidal intent ().

Pillar 1: Scale & Systematic Pattern

Viewing
Para. 342 ↗

Uniform clearance modus operandi across Maungdaw, Buthidaung, and Rathedaung: pre-dawn encirclement, firing on fleeing civilians, sinking Naf River boats, and border landmines ().

Pillar 2: Dehumanizing Rhetoric

Viewing
Para. 412 ↗

Senior officials and State-tolerated campaigns framed the Rohingya as “illegal Bengalis,” invaders, and “unfinished business,” fostering an environment of existential hostility ().

Pillar 3: Targeted Razing of Villages

Viewing
Para. 378 ↗

Over 390 Rohingya villages and mosques were systematically razed while immediately adjacent non-Rohingya settlements and structures were spared ().

Pillar 4: Systemic Impunity & Denial

Viewing
Para. 479 ↗

Total absence of credible domestic prosecutions, blanket official denial, and promotion of implicated commanders confirm genocidal intent as the only reasonable inference ().

Under , counter-insurgency aims do not negate genocidal intent; wholesale attacks on unarmed villages with zero ARSA presence were wildly disproportionate ().

Defense 2: Evidentiary Challenge to UN FFM Reports

Jump to Para. 152 ↗
Myanmar’s Submission

Argued UN Fact-Finding Mission (IIFFMM) reports relied on anonymous refugee accounts and failed to meet the Court’s “fully conclusive” evidentiary bar.

ICJ Ruling

Substantial corroborative weight accorded to IIFFMM and IIMM dossiers where transparently cross-verified by satellite imagery, medical surveys, and consistent testimony (, ).

REJECTED

Defense 3: Absence of a Formal State Plan

Viewing in PDF
Jump to Para. 468 ↗

ICJ Ruling

A written blueprint is unnecessary; synchronized multi-township deployment of elite Light Infantry Divisions refutes rogue-unit claims and proves a coordinated State plan (, ).

3 Rulings
STANDARD OF PROOF

“Fully Conclusive” Proof & UN FFM / IIMM Corroboration

Viewing in PDF
Jump to Para. 134 ↗

Exercising its merits mandate (), the Court reaffirmed that genocide requires fully conclusive proof assessed holistically across direct and circumstantial evidence (), granting high probative weight to corroborated UN FFM and IIMM dossiers ().

BREACH FOUND

Breach of 23 January 2020 Provisional Measures Order

Viewing in PDF
Jump to Para. 504 ↗

Myanmar violated the binding 2020 Order by failing to prevent ongoing genocidal harms, destroying crime-site evidence through bulldozing and military construction over razed Rohingya villages, and failing to submit adequate, verifiable compliance reports ().

REMEDIES ORDERED

State Responsibility, Cessation, Non-Repetition & Reparations

Viewing in PDF
Jump to Para. 521 ↗

Attributing Tatmadaw and Border Guard Police conduct directly to Myanmar (), the Court held Myanmar directly responsible for committing genocide () and ordered cessation, safe voluntary return, non-discriminatory citizenship access, full reparation, and a 2-year compliance report (, ).
